# Academic prototype limitations

- This is a Hindi/Hinglish NLP project with a small Chennai case study. It has no production-service or broad multilingual reliability claim.
- The teaching intent/slot datasets are synthetic and not human reviewed. Reported scores describe these samples and their declared template-family splits. Real Hindi passengers, informal spellings and complex grammar can behave differently.
- MuRIL starts from an existing transport-fine-tuned checkpoint; only its final two layers and a new head are adapted. The TF-IDF baseline can outperform it. This is partial fine-tuning, not a fresh pretrained-model comparison.
- Entity extraction reuses the full canonical name gazetteer and simple Hindi/Hinglish rules. Exact names and stored aliases are supported; every Hindi spelling or Roman transliteration is not guaranteed. Unknown places, duplicate stop names, multiple locations, negation and indirect grammar can require clarification or be misunderstood. Original text, actual name matches and predicted intent remain visible for error analysis.
- The curated case-study facts cover seven locations, four Metro pairs in both directions and two bus snapshot connections. Additional name/route/timetable lookups reuse the existing SQLite service. The snapshot has railway station names but no suburban-rail/MRTS route sequences or schedules. An absent record means missing information, not proof that real service is absent. Bare Park station can refer to Park or Park Town, as well as bus records, and requires qualification.
- Metro facts are published connectivity/facility records. Nearby bus-stop records are snapshot entries. Neither confirms current operation, a complete journey, a walkable transfer or barrier-free access. Central Metro facilities must not be attributed to Central Railway Station.
- The supplementary Central/Velachery guidance names Park Town as a separate MRTS boarding/alighting station. It is published line guidance, not a verified access path, current timetable or optimized journey. Nearby-stop distances are straight-line measurements from source coordinates. Conflicting coordinates, including Guindy Metro, prevent reliable distance estimates; the app can still return official associated stop names when a sourced fallback exists.
- Train-delay/status, ticketing and railway-complaint categories demonstrate recognition and Hindi guidance. The prototype does not track trains, book tickets or file complaints. One 2024 weekday terminal window is a historical illustration; current, destination-specific and railway departure times remain unknown.
- The Hindi–Tamil component is a five-phrase lookup requiring Tamil-speaker review. General translation, Hindi speech recognition and text-to-speech are not implemented in the core demo.
- Template classification/slot scores and the 90-question pipeline diagnostic are separate measurements. The diagnostic is AI-authored and development-exposed; its failures informed a training-data correction. The user's four questions are qualitative checks, not a blind benchmark. Software regression passes do not establish real-user NLP quality or travel-answer accuracy.
- Selected model weights and tokenizer/config assets must be present locally. Generated project head/baseline weights are excluded from Git and can be recreated with the training script.
